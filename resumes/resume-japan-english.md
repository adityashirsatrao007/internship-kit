# English Resume Tuned for Japanese Companies

**Use this for:** Mercari, Rakuten, Sony, LY Corp (LINE+Yahoo), Google Japan, Microsoft Japan, HENNGE, Woven by Toyota, PayPay.

**Why a special version (research from japan-dev.com, paulroberts.jp, GaijinBlog):**
- Most modern/English-first Japanese companies accept a Western-style English resume — **no rirekisho needed**.
- But Japanese recruiters scan for things differently: **Japanese language level, willingness to relocate, and interest in Japan**. A resume that's a generic US resume performs worse.
- Japan allows **2 pages** for tech resumes (North American 1-page rule doesn't apply).

**Key differences from the standard ATS resume:**
1. Add a clear **Languages section** (JLPT N5 → always list it, plus English level).
2. Add a **"Why Japan / Relocation"** line — stands out to Japanese recruiters.
3. Keep 1–2 pages, single-column, same ATS rules.

## Japan-specific tips (researched)
- **Rakuten:** English is corporate language — resume in English is fine, but they like to see you've researched Rakuten's businesses (fintech, e-commerce, mobile).
- **Mercari:** NO fixed format for documents. They explicitly ask for links to GitHub, blog, or past work. They want to see your OUTPUT. Include your GitHub prominently. No separate resume/work-history needed.
- **LY Corp / PayPay:** Japanese resume not required for English tracks; B2 English accepted.
- **Mercari 28卒 internship:** age 16+, no year-of-study restriction — **you can apply as a 1st/2nd year**. Documents: free format, but recommend: background summary + projects + role + results + your goals at Mercari.
- **Never** write "willing to work overtime" or anything implying you'll do menial work — Japanese recruiters don't reward that on resumes.
- Keep every claim **provable** — Japanese hiring places high weight on honesty and accuracy of dates/credentials (they cross-check).
